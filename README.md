# Between the Lines

A simple web page for taking notes on lecture slides.

## The workspace

- **Notebooks**, Notion style: every file you open becomes its own notebook in the **sidebar** (left), with its own notes, Back / Forward history and place on the page. Click a notebook to switch; the one you're in shows thumbnails of its pages. **New notebook** starts one with a blank page, and each notebook's **⋯** renames or deletes it (double-click the name in the title bar to rename too). **Insert › Add a file here** adds a file to the notebook you're in instead of opening a new one. Hide the sidebar with **«** (Ctrl+\\) and point at the left edge of the window to peek at it.
- **Split view**: read a second notebook beside the one you're writing in. Use **Open beside** in a notebook's **⋯** menu, drag the notebook onto the right half of the page, or press Ctrl+K and choose **Open beside…** (Alt+Enter on a notebook result). The notebook beside is read-only: you can scroll it, jump to a page, point at its pencil icons and copy its text. Drag the divider to resize the two halves; **Swap** switches which notebook you write in, and **×** closes it. On a narrow screen it opens as a drawer.
- **Title bar**: the notebook's name and a *Saved in this browser* indicator, Back / Forward (undo and redo), then **Open** and **Download** (your files), **Focus** and **Study mode** (how you work), and Help. The buttons at each end show or hide the sidebar and the **Notes** panel.
- **Sidebar actions**: **New notebook**, **New folder**, **Open a file**, then the cloud rows together under *Open from the cloud* (**Google Drive**, **Your Drive notebooks**, **OneDrive**, **Your OneDrive notebooks**), **Add a note** and **Search**.
- **Toolbar tabs**, like Word's ribbon:
  - **Home**: font, text size (type any size and press Enter, or pick one), colours and favourite colours, bold / italic / underline / strikethrough, superscript and subscript, highlighter, clear formatting, bullet and numbered lists (bullet style and indent size menus; or type `- `, `* ` or `1. ` at the start of a line, and Back if you meant the characters) and alignment.
  - **Insert**: a sticky note, formulas, pictures / videos / sound, **Writing tools** (the writing guide, tables, journal entries and the **Templates** gallery), a blank page after the current page or another file added to this notebook, and, on its own at the far end, **Remove page**.
  - **Box**: a note box's outline and fill, the colour of its minimised icon, **Select all**, **Align** (see below) and **Auto-align notes**.
  - **View**, most used first: page layout (Default, A4 or A3, portrait or landscape); **Show** (**Hide notes**, a menu to hide the notes on your pages only while side page notes stay open, hide every note, or show them all; the Sidebar and Notes list); **Side pages** (Side notes, Sync scrolling, Link lines and **Side page background**, which shows a small swatch of the paper); Theme; spell check language; toolbar size (**Full**, **Tabs** or **Hidden**); **Show tips**; and, apart at the end, **Clear notebook**. On narrower windows the View buttons keep their icons and lose their words, and the tab scrolls sideways when it still doesn't fit (a thin scroll bar shows when you point at the toolbar or scroll it, and a soft fade marks the side that has more). **Open PowerPoint as** is in Help & settings.
- **Notes panel** (right): write a note at the top and press **Add note** (or Enter) to put it at the bottom of the page you're on, or **Sticky** to stick it on that page; drag **Sticky** onto any page to put it exactly there. Below that, every note in the notebook, listed by page: search them, filter by icon colour, and click one to jump to it (a minimised note opens). **Add a note** in the sidebar opens it ready to write.
- **Status bar**: which page you're on, how many notes there are (pinned, minimised), and Hide / Show notes.
- **Side pages**: **View › Side notes** gives each slide a page for your own notes beside it, in the Default layout and in A4 and A3 too (each printed page gets its own side page beside it, and the two shrink together when there is not room; they are saved, and added to the PDF, in every layout). Point near the top right corner of a side page (or tap it, or press Tab) and a small palette button fades in to change its paper; **Side page background** in the View tab and Ctrl+K do the same.
- **Scaling**: a slide (or printed page), its side page and all its notes are laid out at one fixed size and scaled to fit the room, so they look the same, just smaller, when you open the sidebar or the Notes list, turn on side notes or make the window narrower: notes keep their place and their text wraps the same way. Only a phone-sized column is left to flow.
- **Tips and tooltips**: **Show tips** (View tab, Help & settings or Ctrl+K) turns the short hints that teach a feature on or off, for example how to leave Focus mode; what happened (Saved, errors, Drive and OneDrive messages) always shows. **Button tooltips** (Help & settings or Ctrl+K) turns off the small note that pops up when you point at a button; screen readers still read it.
- **Focus mode** (**Focus** in the title bar, or Ctrl+Shift+F) hides the toolbar, both side panels and the status bar so only your pages and notes are on screen. **Exit focus** (top right) or **Esc** brings everything back. You can also close each panel on its own with its ×, or shrink the toolbar to tabs only with the arrow at the end of the tabs.
- The look is pencil on paper: hand-drawn borders, a handwritten interface font and a dotted paper background (a chalkboard in dark mode). Your slides stay exactly as they are.
- On a phone the panels open as drawers over the page, and the toolbar tabs scroll sideways.

## Taking notes

- **Open** a **PDF**, or drop it anywhere on the page. Each page shows exactly as it is, with background graphics, side bands and slide numbers removed. You can select and copy text from the pages. A scanned PDF (handwritten notes from a scanner or a scanner app, one picture per page) shows its pictures, with **+ Add here** finding the blank rows of the paper as it does on a picture; if the scan has recognised text, that text can be selected too.
- Point at a spot on a page and press **+ Add here** to add a **Note** there: the page opens up at the nearest gap between lines for you to type in your own font, size and colour (handwriting styles and standard fonts). **Right-click** the button (or the dashed line; press and hold the button on a touch screen) for a menu like Notion's: **Note**, **Sticky note** (put on top of the page there) or **Equation** (a formula in the gap). Point at an option or use the arrow keys, type to search, Enter to add, Esc to close.
- In a note, type **/** (at the start, or after a space) for the same menu at the cursor: add an equation, or turn the note into a sticky note and back. What you type after the slash searches it.
- **Sticky notes** are yellow paper on top of the page. Drag one by its tape to move it anywhere, even onto another page; it shows in the Notes panel as a sticky note and in the PDF as you see it.
- **PowerPoint (.pptx)** files open as the slides look: each slide is drawn with its positioned text, theme colours and fonts, backgrounds, pictures, shapes, tables, charts, grouped shapes and SmartArt, then shown like a PDF page (a picture with its text on top you can select and copy), so notes go between the lines exactly as on a PDF. Complex animations and SmartArt may still differ, and fonts that aren't on your device are replaced by similar ones; for an exact copy, save the slides as a PDF (File › Save as › PDF) and open that. Choose **Open PowerPoint as › Editable text** (View tab, Help & settings or the welcome page) to rebuild the slides as text you can type inside, as before; the tip under a file you've just opened can reopen it the other way. A slide that can't be drawn is rebuilt as text on its own. An animated GIF on a slide moves too, as long as it is shown upright and uncropped; otherwise it stays on its first frame.
- **Word (.docx)** files open as editable text that keeps their fonts, colours, tables, lists, headers and pictures, one page for each page break, and you can type inside sentences too. They are drawn with [docx-preview](https://github.com/VolodymyrBaydalka/docxjs) (loaded the first time you open a Word file, then kept for offline use); if it can't load, the plainer reader is used. A few pictures (old EMF or WMF drawings) may not come through.
- **Pictures** (a scan or a photo of handwritten notes: .jpg, .png, .webp, .gif or .bmp) open as pages, just like a PDF page: as wide as the other pages, in the picture's own shape, with notes between its lines (**+ Add here** finds the blank rows of the paper, even inside a table drawn by hand, and never opens the page through a line of writing), floating notes, sticky notes and drawing on top, in the PDF and on Drive and OneDrive too. A picture dropped *into* a note stays a picture in that note. There are no words to select on a picture page. An animated GIF keeps moving on its page (and in a note); the downloaded PDF shows its first frame.
- Notes sit in boxes. A box's handles show only when you point at it (or tap it on a touch screen), so the page stays clear. Drag any **edge or corner** to resize it: the side you drag moves and the opposite side stays where it is (a floating note or sticky note changes its size and place; a note between the lines changes its width and left margin, and from the top its top margin). Drag the move handle to move it across the page or between other lines. Pink guide lines show when it lines up with other notes; it gently snaps only within a few pixels, and holding **Alt** or **Shift** turns snapping off. With a finger the handles of a note you have tapped get a bigger catching area; the Apple Pencil writes instead of dragging (unless *Apple Pencil draws automatically* is off).
- **Auto-align**: a new note between the lines lines up with the left edge of the nearest note above or below it on the page, when one is within about 40 px (its right edge stays put). Turn it off with **Box › Auto-align notes** (or Ctrl+K).
- **Align** (Box tab) works on the notes you selected (**Select all**, or Ctrl+A), or the note you are in: **left**, **centre** or **right** (on the leftmost, middle or rightmost edge), **Same width** (as wide as the widest), **Distribute** (three or more floating notes or sticky notes, spaced evenly from the top one to the bottom one) and **Tidy page** (every note between the lines on the page lines up with the leftmost one, in the same order; floating notes stay where they are). Each is one step for Back.
- Each note's handles: **float** it on top of the page (like "in front of text" in Word), **minimise** it to a small pencil icon (it shows the note when you point at it, opens when clicked, and can be dragged aside), and **pin** it so it stays open.
- **Colour-code** the pencil icons by topic: right-click one (press and hold on a touch screen), or use **Box › Icon colours**. Save **favourite colours** with the star next to the text colour.
- **Writing guide** (Insert tab) puts a grid, ruled lines or dots over the pages so you can write in straight lines with a pen or Apple Pencil. The little bar on it switches the pattern, changes the spacing (12 to 48 px) and the opacity, and closes it (× or Esc). Drag its handle to put it anywhere, even on another page (hold it at the bottom of the window and the page scrolls along), and pull any edge or corner to resize it; the handles are bigger on a touch screen. It lets pen, ink and notes through, stays put on the page as you scroll, and is only for now: it isn't saved in the notebook, the history, the PDF or Drive. Only the last pattern, spacing and opacity are remembered. Also in Ctrl+K.
- **Table** (Insert tab, **+ Add here** or `/table`) opens a Word-style grid: point or drag over up to 10 × 8 squares ("3 × 4" shows as you go) and click, or use the arrow keys and Enter, or **Custom size…**. It goes into the note you're in at the cursor, or into a new note in the nearest gap. Tab moves to the next cell (Tab in the last cell adds a row), drag a column border to change the widths, and drag the right edge or the corner to size the table. Right-click a cell (or use the **⋯** at its corner; press and hold on a touch screen) to insert or delete rows and columns, merge or split cells, or delete the table. Drag across cells, or Shift+click, to select several (Delete empties them). Tables are saved with the notes and appear in the PDF and the notebook file.
- **Journal entry** (Insert tab, **+ Add here**, `/journal` or `/je`, or Ctrl+K) is an accounting entry lined up in four invisible columns: DR/CR, account, debit and credit amounts. The date (today's, editable) is on the first line and a faint description on the last. Click DR or CR (or type dr / cr) to switch a row, Enter adds a row on the same side, Backspace in an empty row removes it, and Tab moves between the fields. Amounts get thousands separators when you leave them, and a small "≠ 1,200" appears beside the description if debits and credits don't match. In the dark theme it sits on a dark panel; the PDF uses colours for paper.
- **Templates** (Insert tab, **+ Add here › Template…**, `/template` or `/t`, or Ctrl+K › Templates…) opens a gallery with the subjects down the left (Recent, Favourites, General study, Accounting & Finance, Economics, Mathematics, Statistics, Physics, Chemistry, Biology, Computer Science, Languages, Law, Medicine & Health), a search box, and cards with a name, a one-line description, a small live preview and a star to favourite. Click or press Enter to put one in: it goes into the note you're in at the cursor, or into a new note in the nearest gap. Arrow keys move between cards, Esc closes, and the last subject and the recent and favourite ones are remembered. On a phone the subjects become a row of chips. Ctrl+K also finds a template by its name or subject (try `punnett` or `ice table`) and puts it straight in. 46 templates:
  - *General study*: Cornell notes, Definition card, Flashcard (in Study mode, click the answer to show it), Pros and cons, Compare and contrast, Timeline, Key takeaways (a checklist).
  - *Accounting & Finance*: Journal entry (the existing one; its button and `/je` still work), **T-account** (Enter adds a row on that side; the balance shows as Bal c/d and Bal b/d on the right sides), **Trial balance** (totals add up, a small ≠ mark when they differ), Bank reconciliation, Depreciation schedule (straight line), NPV and cash flows.
  - *Economics*: Supply and demand (drag the curves or their ends, or use the shift buttons; the old curve stays dashed), Elasticity calculation (midpoint method), Comparative advantage.
  - *Mathematics*: Matrix (choose a size, Tab between entries, three bracket styles), Two-column proof, Piecewise function, System of equations, Worked solution, Graph paper (axes with ticks; draw with a pen, or with a mouse or finger after pressing Draw).
  - *Statistics*: Hypothesis test, Frequency table (relative and cumulative frequency), Summary statistics (type or paste numbers).
  - *Physics*: Problem layout, SUVAT table (give any three, the other two are worked out), Unit converter, Free-body diagram.
  - *Chemistry*: Chemical equation (type `2H2 + O2 -> 2H2O`: subscripts, arrows, states, charges, and a balance check), ICE table, Stoichiometry steps, Element lookup (all 118 elements; a formula gives its molar mass).
  - *Biology*: Punnett square (type `Aa` and `Aa`, or `AaBb` and `AaBb`), Experiment write-up, Labelled diagram.
  - *Computer Science*: Code block (language picker, light colouring, Tab puts in spaces), Truth table (type `A and (B or not C)`), Big-O comparison, Pseudocode.
  - *Languages*: Vocabulary table, Verb conjugation. *Law*: IRAC, Case brief. *Medicine & Health*: SOAP note, Drug card.
  Smart blocks work things out as you type and keep the answers in the note, so they are saved, undone and redone with everything else, and are in the PDF and notebook file. Many templates are ordinary tables, so rows, columns and widths can be changed. Fields are big enough for a finger at `pointer: coarse`, Scribble works in them, and in Study mode they can't be changed.
- The **Formulas** tab of the same gallery holds about 120 common formulas (finance, economics, mathematics, statistics, physics, chemistry, biology, computer science, medicine), each with what its symbols mean, a subject filter and a search. Click one to put it in as a normal formula you can click and edit. The formula editor also has a **Ω…** button with more Greek letters and symbols.
- **Formula** inserts a formula: type it plainly, e.g. `w_i = (P_i)/(sum_(i=1)^N P_i)`, or use the symbol buttons, with a live preview. Click a formula to edit it.
- **Picture, video or sound** inserts media into a note; you can also paste or drop pictures straight into a note. Click a picture or video to resize it, add a note under it, or remove it.
- **Spell check**: misspelt words get a red wavy line; right-click one (or tap it) for suggestions, **Add to dictionary** or **Ignore**. It checks against the LibreOffice US or UK English word lists plus a list of academic and technical words, and also accepts prefixed and hyphenated words (re-rating, non-linear), names and brands, e-mail and web addresses, file names and acronyms, so mostly real slips are marked. Suggestions allow for neighbouring keys, swapped letters, doubled letters and sound-alikes, and put the commoner word first. The word list is made once, on your first visit, and kept on the device (the internet is needed only that first time). **Spelling: device** (View tab) leaves checking to your browser or system instead (a good choice on a Mac or iPad). Your dictionary and favourite colours are in **Help & settings**.
- **Select all** (Ctrl+A outside a note) selects every note on the page to restyle, move (drag or arrow keys), line up or delete together.
- **Back / Forward** (Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y) undo and redo any change.
- **Draw** (Insert tab) opens a dark floating toolbar for PDF pages and side-note pages: **Pen**, **Pencil** (a soft, grainy line), **Highlighter** (see-through, and no darker where a stroke crosses itself), **Text** (tap the page to type there in the colour, size and font you pick) and **Eraser** (whole strokes, or **Partial** to rub out only where it goes, like a real eraser). Each tool keeps its own colour (five, or any colour you like) and thickness, set with a slider in millimetres (a CSS millimetre of the page as it is laid out, so a 0.5mm pen looks the same on every page and grows with the zoom). **Save as favourite** keeps the tool, colour and thickness one tap away (right-click a favourite, or press and hold it, to remove it), and **Minimise** folds the toolbar into a small purple button while you carry on drawing. Everything is remembered after a reload. Strokes are saved with the notebook, show in Study mode, sync and come out in the PDF, and Back undoes them.
- **Study mode** makes everything read-only while you revise; the editing tabs are put away and notes can still be opened and minimised.

## On iPad and tablets

- **Add to Home Screen** (in Safari: Share, then Add to Home Screen) and it opens full screen like an app, and keeps working offline. Safari on an iPad or iPhone shows a small card with these steps once (again two weeks later if you close it, and never after you close it twice; not at all with Show tips off). **Install as an app** in Help & settings shows them any time.
- **Apple Pencil** (and other pens, such as a Surface pen) draws on a PDF page or a side page with the tool from **Draw**, even when Draw is off, while your fingers scroll, tap and select as usual. A hand resting on the screen while you write is ignored. Pressing harder makes the pen and pencil lines thicker (the highlighter stays flat), and the Pencil's eraser end rubs strokes out where the browser reports it. A quick tap with the Pencil is a tap, not a dot: it shows **+ Add here**. Turn this off with **Apple Pencil draws automatically** in Help & settings, and the Pencil then draws only while **Draw** is on. To draw with a finger, turn on **Draw**.
- **Scribble**: tap inside a note and write with the Pencil, and iPadOS turns it into text (switch Scribble on in Settings › Apple Pencil). The Pencil never draws over a note, so you can always write in one.
- **Press and hold** is the touch version of right-click: on **+ Add here** (sticky note or equation), on a pencil icon (its colours), on a misspelt word (suggestions; a tap works too) and on a notebook in the sidebar (its menu). Tap a note to show its handles for moving and resizing.
- **Tap with two fingers** on the page to go **Back** one step (undo), as in GoodNotes. A pinch, a two-finger scroll or two fingers held down are left alone.
- Held upright, the sidebar and the Notes list open over the pages as drawers (tap outside to close them), so the pages keep their size; turned sideways they sit beside the pages again.
- Buttons, tabs and menu entries grow to fingertip size (44 px) on touch screens, and the page keeps your place when you turn the iPad round. The keyboard never covers the note you are typing in, and Safari does not zoom in when you tap into a note or a box.
- Made light for Safari: no paper-grain layer over the screen, no grey flash or double-tap zoom on what you tap, no buttons left lit after a tap, nothing on the page that holds up scrolling, and each PDF page's drawing is let go of as soon as it is stored (a long PDF no longer runs Safari out of canvas memory). It is made to run on Safari 15 and later (iPadOS and iOS 15).

## Saving

- Everything is saved automatically in your browser; the title bar says if the browser's storage is full or blocked.
- **Download** a **PDF** (in the layout you picked, with notes on the page, as clickable PDF comments, or left out) or a **notebook file** (one .html file you can open later on any device, or with Open, to keep editing). Ctrl+S opens the Download menu. The same menu can also save them to Google Drive or OneDrive (see below).

## Google Drive

Notebooks can be opened from, saved to, and shared through Google Drive. The site owner registers the site with Google once (see the set-up below); after that, every visitor signs in with their own Google account, and their files stay in their own Drive. It only works on the hosted site (https://ylp-games.github.io/Between-the-lines/, or `http://localhost:8000` while testing), not on a file opened from disk and not in a preview. The page asks for the `drive.file` permission only: it can reach files you pick with Google's file picker and files it saves itself, never the rest of your Drive. With that permission the page can also share files it saved or that you picked. Until the site owner does the set-up below, the Drive buttons show a message saying Drive isn't set up yet.

- **Google Drive** under *Open from the cloud* in the sidebar (or the link on the welcome screen): pick one or more PDF, PowerPoint, Word, Markdown or text files, pictures, notebook `.html` files, Google Slides (opened as a PDF) or Google Docs (opened as a Word file). Each becomes its own notebook. The picker has tabs for **My Drive** (open folders to browse, and Shared drives), **Shared with me**, **Recent** and **Starred**, so files other people have shared with you can be opened too.
- **Download › Notebook to Drive** and **PDF to Drive**: the first time this makes `<name> (notes).html` (or `.pdf`) in the folder the file was opened from; after that it updates that same file. If the file has been deleted from Drive, a new one is made.
- **Download › Share…** (or **Share via Google Drive…** in a notebook's ⋯ menu in the sidebar): saves the notebook file to Drive first (or the PDF, if you choose that in the dialog), then opens a small dialog. **Add people** by email (several, separated by commas) as Viewer, Commenter or Editor, with an optional email notice and message; see **People with access** and remove anyone but the owner; set **General access** to Restricted or Anyone with the link (Viewer or Commenter); **Copy link** or **Open in Drive**. People you share the notebook file with can open it in Between the Lines (**Open › From Google Drive**) to see your notes, or download it. If Drive refuses (for example, your organisation doesn't allow sharing outside it), the dialog says why.
- **A notebook shared with you to view only**: opening its `.html` file from **Shared with me** works as normal. Since you can't change the original, **Notebook to Drive** saves a copy called `<name> (my copy).html` in your own Drive instead, and says so.
- **Help & settings › Google Drive** shows who is connected and has a **Disconnect** button.

### Set-up (for the site owner, once)

1. In the [Google Cloud console](https://console.cloud.google.com/), create a project.
2. Under **APIs & Services › Library**, enable the **Google Drive API** and the **Google Picker API**.
3. Under **OAuth consent screen**, choose **External**, fill in the app name and your email, and add the scope `https://www.googleapis.com/auth/drive.file`. Then decide who can sign in: while the app's publishing status is *Testing*, only the Google accounts listed as **test users** (at most 100) can sign in, and everyone else is turned away. To let anyone sign in, press **Publish app** so that it shows **In production**. `drive.file` is a non-sensitive scope, so Google does not ask for its full security review; you only need the app name, a support email and the consent-screen details filled in.
4. Under **Credentials › Create credentials › OAuth client ID**, choose **Web application** and add these **Authorised JavaScript origins**: `https://ylp-games.github.io` (and `http://localhost:8000` for testing). No redirect URIs are needed.
5. Under **Credentials › Create credentials › API key**, restrict it to **Websites** (HTTP referrer) `https://ylp-games.github.io/*` (and `http://localhost:8000/*` for testing), and under **API restrictions** to the **Google Picker API**.
6. Paste both into `GDRIVE` at the top of the script in `index.html`, then push:

   ```js
   const GDRIVE = {clientId: '1234567890-abc….apps.googleusercontent.com', apiKey: 'AIza…'};
   ```

Both values are public by design (they are in every page that uses Google sign-in); the origin and referrer restrictions are what protect them. The Picker's app id is worked out from the client ID (the number before the first dash).

## OneDrive and Microsoft 365

Notebooks can also be opened from, saved to, and shared through OneDrive and SharePoint, with a personal Microsoft account or a work or school (Microsoft 365) account. As with Google Drive, the site owner registers the site with Microsoft once (see the set-up below); after that, every visitor signs in with their own account, and their files stay in their own OneDrive. It only works on the hosted site (https://ylp-games.github.io/Between-the-lines/, or `http://localhost:8123` while testing), not on a file opened from disk. Sign-in is a pop-up from Microsoft (Microsoft's MSAL.js library, loaded only when you press a OneDrive button), so allow pop-ups for the site. Until the site owner does the set-up below, the OneDrive buttons show a message saying OneDrive isn't set up yet.

- **OneDrive** under *Open from the cloud* in the sidebar (or the link on the welcome screen): opens a small file browser with tabs for **My files** (open folders; a path above the list takes you back up), **Shared with me** and **Recent**, and a search box. It shows folders and the files this page can open: PDF, PowerPoint, Word, Markdown or text files, pictures and notebook `.html` files. Click a file to tick it (several can be ticked) and press **Open**, or press Enter. The arrow keys move through the list, Space ticks, Enter opens a file or goes into a folder, Backspace goes up a folder, and Esc closes it. Each file becomes its own notebook.
- **Open PowerPoint/Word as it looks (PDF)**: a box in the file browser, on by default and remembered. OneDrive turns `.pptx` and `.docx` files into a PDF, so every page looks just as it does in Office; the notebook is named after the file. If OneDrive can't make the PDF, the original file is opened instead and a short message says so. Untick it to open the original file and its text.
- **Download › Save to OneDrive**: the first time this makes `<name> (notes).html` in a `Between the Lines` folder in your OneDrive (never over a file that is already there); after that it updates that same file. A notebook opened from a OneDrive `.html` file updates that file. If the file has been deleted, a new one is made. Large notebooks (over 4 MB) are uploaded in pieces, with the progress shown.
- **Keeping a notebook in sync**: once a notebook has been saved to OneDrive (or opened from a OneDrive `.html` file), it keeps itself up to date there, exactly as notebooks in Google Drive do. About 5 seconds after you stop writing (and at least once a minute while you keep going) the changes are uploaded to the same file: one upload, only when something changed. The button at the top shows the state: *Synced to OneDrive*, *Syncing soon*, *Syncing…*, *Syncs when online* (offline; it goes up when you're back), *Sync paused* (click to try again), *Sign in to sync*, *Changed in two places*, *Not in OneDrive* and *Can't sync*. Changes made on another device are fetched when you open the notebook, when you come back to the tab, and about every 45 seconds, and Back brings your earlier version back. Before every upload the file's version (its `cTag`) is compared with the one this notebook last saw. If someone changed it since and you have changes waiting too, nothing is overwritten: the button turns to *Changed in two places* and offers **This version**, **OneDrive's version** or **Keep both** (your version becomes a separate notebook). Syncing never opens a Microsoft window by itself: it renews the sign-in quietly, and if that isn't possible (a new browser tab, or the sign-in has run out) the button says *Sign in to sync* and one click signs in and syncs. If the file is deleted or in the recycle bin it says *Not in OneDrive* and a click saves it again as a new file; if you can no longer change it (*Can't sync*) a click saves a copy in your own OneDrive. When Microsoft says to slow down, the upload waits as long as it asks and tries again.
- **One cloud at a time**: a notebook follows the cloud it was saved to last. If you save a notebook that syncs with Google Drive to OneDrive (or the other way round), it moves to the new one as a new file and a message says so; the file in the old cloud stays as it was and is no longer updated.
- **Your OneDrive notebooks** under *Open from the cloud* in the sidebar (also in Search, Ctrl+K): lists the `.html` notebooks in the `Between the Lines` folder of your OneDrive, plus any notebook on this device that was opened from elsewhere in OneDrive, newest first, from any device. Pick one to carry on where you left off. A notebook already on this device is switched to and brought up to date, not copied, and one that isn't is downloaded and kept in sync from then on.
- **Download › Share via OneDrive…** (or **Share via OneDrive…** in a notebook's ⋯ menu in the sidebar): saves the notebook to OneDrive first, then opens the same sharing dialog as Google Drive. **Add people** by email (several, separated by commas) as Viewer or Editor, with an optional email and message; see **People with access** and remove anyone but the owner (access that comes from the folder can only be changed there); set **General access** to Restricted or Anyone with the link (Viewer or Editor); **Copy link** or **Open in OneDrive**. If your organisation doesn't allow links for anyone, a link for people in your organisation is made instead and the dialog says why.
- **A notebook shared with you to view only**: opening its `.html` file from **Shared with me** works as normal. Since you can't change the original, **Save to OneDrive** saves a copy called `<name> (my copy).html` in your own OneDrive instead, and says so.
- **Help & settings › OneDrive and Microsoft 365** shows who is signed in and has a **Sign out** button. The sign-in is kept for the tab only.

### Set-up (for the site owner, once)

1. Sign in to the [Microsoft Entra admin center](https://entra.microsoft.com/) with any Microsoft account (a free Azure account is enough) and go to **Entra ID › App registrations › New registration**.
2. Give it a name (for example "Between the Lines"). Under **Supported account types** choose **Accounts in any organizational directory and personal Microsoft accounts**, so that school, work and personal accounts can all sign in.
3. Under **Redirect URI**, choose the platform **Single-page application (SPA)** and enter `https://ylp-games.github.io/Between-the-lines/msal-redirect.html` (this is the blank page in the repository that the sign-in pop-up returns to). Press **Register**. Then, under **Authentication**, add a second redirect URI of the same kind, `http://localhost:8123/msal-redirect.html`, if you want to test on your own computer. It must be a *Single-page application* redirect, not *Web*.
4. Under **API permissions › Add a permission › Microsoft Graph › Delegated permissions**, add **User.Read** and **Files.ReadWrite.All** (the second is what lets people open files that others have shared with them). Neither is an admin-only permission by default, so people can agree to it themselves when they sign in (unless their organisation says otherwise, see below).
5. Copy the **Application (client) ID** from the app's **Overview** page and paste it into `ONEDRIVE` at the top of the script in `index.html`, then push:

   ```js
   const ONEDRIVE = {clientId: '00000000-0000-0000-0000-000000000000'};
   ```

The client ID is public by design (it is in every page that uses Microsoft sign-in). **No client secret is needed. Never create one for this app and never put one in the code or the repository**: a single-page app can't keep a secret, and anything in the page is public. Security comes from the redirect URI, which Microsoft checks exactly.

Some school and work organisations don't let their members use apps they haven't approved. If someone gets "Need admin approval" when signing in, an administrator of their organisation has to approve the app once (in Entra: **Enterprise applications › Consent and permissions**, or by following the link Microsoft shows to request approval); the page then says "Your organisation needs an administrator to approve this app". Personal Microsoft accounts are not affected.

## Use it

Open it at **https://ylp-games.github.io/Between-the-lines/**, or open `index.html` in a browser. The app is a single file with no build step.

**Install it as an app**: press **Install as an app** in Help & settings (or at the bottom of the sidebar) for the steps in the browser you are using.

- **iPad and iPhone**: in Safari, tap **Share** (at the top right on an iPad, at the bottom on an iPhone), choose **Add to Home Screen**, then **Add**. It opens full screen from its icon and works offline.
- **Chrome or Edge** (computer or Android): **Install as an app** opens the browser's own install prompt (or use the install icon in the address bar). It then opens in its own window, works offline, and appears in "Open with" for PDF, PowerPoint and Word files.
- **Safari on a Mac** (17 or later): File › Add to Dock.

`manifest.webmanifest`, `sw.js` (the offline cache) and `icons/` (with `apple-touch-icon.png`, the Home Screen icon for iOS) make this work; they are only used when the site is served from the web.

Pushing to `main` publishes the site to GitHub Pages automatically (`.github/workflows/static.yml`).
